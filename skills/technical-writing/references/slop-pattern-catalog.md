# Slop Pattern and Language Catalog

Use this catalogue for drafting, material revision, humanizing, and AI-slop
audits. It consolidates the recurring patterns and watched language from the
skill's grounded research.

Avoid a catalogued item most of the time when plain, specific prose can perform
the same job. Keep it when it is accurate, necessary, quoted, canonical,
natural to the supplied author, or clearly better for the reader.

## Apply the catalogue

- Treat **default avoid** items as a presumption to revise.
- Treat **density watch** items as a problem when several appear in one
  paragraph or the same move recurs across sections.
- Treat **context only** items as inspection prompts. Never flag them alone.
- Protect quotations, titles, code, commands, identifiers, proper names,
  citations, interface text, and examples that discuss the watched language.
- Diagnose the empty function and reader harm. Do not diagnose AI authorship.
- Prefer deletion or a specific fact over automatic synonym replacement.

## Content and claim patterns

- **S01 Significance inflation:** Claim that a routine fact marks a pivotal
  moment, plays a vital role, leaves a legacy, or represents a broader shift.
- **S02 Notability name-dropping:** List publications, awards, followers, or
  media appearances instead of saying what a source established.
- **S03 Promotional description:** Substitute brochure adjectives,
  superlatives, prestige, or excitement for observable capabilities.
- **S04 Generic landscape framing:** Open with broad claims about a fast-moving
  world, industry, era, ecosystem, or technological landscape.
- **S05 Superficial participle analysis:** Append `-ing` clauses such as
  “highlighting” or “ensuring” that restate rather than explain a consequence.
- **S06 Symbolic gloss:** Tell the reader that a fact reflects, embodies,
  symbolizes, or speaks to a larger meaning without support.
- **S07 Vague attribution:** Invoke experts, research, observers, critics,
  reports, or consensus without a named and scoped source.
- **S08 Source-listing as content:** Treat a pile of citations or outlets as
  proof without connecting a source to the claim it supports.
- **S09 Formulaic challenges section:** Add generic challenges, legacy, future
  outlook, opportunities, or road-ahead material without specific evidence.
- **S10 Unsupported prediction:** Promise growth, transformation, future
  success, continuing influence, or exciting developments without a source.
- **S11 Hallucinated specificity:** Supply an unverified date, number, quote,
  citation, benchmark, name, or obscure factual detail.
- **S12 Speculative gap-filling:** Turn missing information into a plausible
  biography, motive, private preference, or causal story.
- **S13 Knowledge-cutoff disclaimer:** Narrate a training date or limited
  knowledge instead of stating what is verified, unknown, or omitted.
- **S14 Generic overview:** Announce comprehensive coverage or summarize a
  topic without a defined reader outcome.
- **S15 Low information density:** Restate one idea through several sentences,
  mini-summaries, or paraphrases that add no new claim.
- **S16 Diff-anchored writing:** Describe what was added, changed, updated, or
  replaced when durable documentation should describe the current state.
- **S17 Generic balance:** Manufacture symmetrical benefits, drawbacks, and a
  neutral middle when the evidence supports a clearer conclusion.
- **S18 Unsupported universality:** Use “always,” “never,” “everyone,” “no
  one,” or “all” when the evidence covers a narrower population.
- **S19 Abstract benefit:** Claim better efficiency, productivity, experience,
  innovation, quality, or performance without naming the changed behavior.
- **S20 Unnecessary context:** Include generic history or background that does
  not change what the reader understands or does.

## Rhetorical and sentence patterns

- **S21 Copula avoidance:** Replace plain `is`, `are`, or `has` with “serves
  as,” “stands as,” “boasts,” “features,” or “offers” without added meaning.
- **S22 Nominalization:** Hide the actor and action inside nouns such as
  “implementation,” “utilization,” “facilitation,” or “optimization.”
- **S23 Synonym cycling:** Rename one entity or technical term merely to avoid
  repetition.
- **S24 Binary contrast:** Repeatedly use “not X but Y,” “not just X,” or “the
  question is not X” instead of stating the point directly.
- **S25 Negative listing:** Stack “not this, not that” fragments or append
  clipped negations such as “no guessing” and “no wasted motion.”
- **S26 Forced triad:** Package unrelated or abstract ideas into groups of
  three to simulate completeness.
- **S27 False range:** Use “from X to Y” when X and Y are not endpoints on one
  meaningful dimension.
- **S28 Faux insight:** Frame a routine claim as what nobody tells you, what
  everyone misses, or the uncomfortable truth.
- **S29 Engagement hook:** Use “the catch?”, “the kicker?”, “sound familiar?”,
  “plot twist,” or similar social-copy bait before an ordinary point.
- **S30 Colon reveal:** Use a noun phrase and colon to manufacture drama before
  a lowercase reveal.
- **S31 Self-answered question:** Ask a rhetorical question and immediately
  answer it when a direct statement is clearer.
- **S32 Signposting announcement:** Announce that the text will dive in,
  explore, break down, unpack, or show what the reader needs to know.
- **S33 Hedged enumeration:** Start with “there are several ways,” “a few
  things to consider,” or another vague list announcement.
- **S34 Formal-register inflation:** Use bureaucratic ceremony where direct
  technical language would be clearer.
- **S35 Stacked hedging:** Combine several uncertainty markers instead of
  stating the one real qualification.
- **S36 Manufactured punchline:** Stack short declarative fragments so every
  sentence sounds like a quotable conclusion.
- **S37 Aphorism formula:** Use “X is the Y of Z,” “X is the new Y,” “the
  currency of,” “where X meets Y,” or a tool-versus-mirror metaphor.
- **S38 Fake-candid opener:** Start with a theatrical “honestly?”, “look,”
  “real talk,” “here's the thing,” or “let's be honest.”
- **S39 Summary-recap ending:** Restate the piece under “in conclusion,”
  “overall,” “in summary,” or “to sum up.”
- **S40 Whether recap:** Close a paragraph with “whether you...” or “whether
  it's...” followed by a generic summary.
- **S41 Generic positive ending:** End with a bright future, exciting times,
  continuing journey, step in the right direction, or call to embrace change.
- **S42 Manufactured profundity:** Add a cute metaphor, moral, deep takeaway,
  or mic-drop line after the last concrete point.
- **S43 Fragmented header:** Follow a heading with one sentence that restates
  it before the actual content.
- **S44 Interchangeable paragraphs:** Build self-contained mini-essays that can
  be reordered without affecting the argument.
- **S45 Uniform rhythm:** Repeat the same sentence length, opening, clause
  pattern, or paragraph shape until the prose sounds mechanical.
- **S46 Hidden or distant actor:** Use vague people, one, society, an
  agentless passive, or a human-like abstraction when a specific actor or the
  reader belongs in the claim. Keep ordinary technical agency when accurate.
- **S47 Transition stacking:** Rotate through additionally, moreover,
  furthermore, consequently, and similarly when the logical relation is
  already clear.
- **S48 Repeated throat-clearing:** Delay the point with meta-commentary,
  generic context, self-positioning, or a declaration of importance.

## Organization and formatting patterns

- **S49 Structured-list syndrome:** Turn connected prose into many bullets with
  bold inline labels and colons.
- **S50 Decorative headings:** Add headings for one or two sentences, duplicate
  the opening sentence, or create symmetry the content does not need.
- **S51 Title-case everywhere:** Capitalize every major word when the
  destination style uses sentence case.
- **S52 Decorative emphasis:** Scatter bold, italics, emoji, horizontal rules,
  callouts, and capitalization instead of establishing real hierarchy.
- **S53 Gratuitous table:** Use a table for prose, incomparable items, or a
  relationship that requires explanation.
- **S54 Question-heading template:** Turn every section into an FAQ-style
  question without a reader-driven reason.
- **S55 Markdown bleed:** Leave Markdown syntax in email, plain text, a word
  processor, or another destination that will not render it.
- **S56 Hyphenated-pair overuse:** Keep compound modifiers hyphenated after the
  noun or hyphenate ordinary phrases mechanically.
- **S57 Punctuation rhythm crutch:** Repeat em dashes, colons, semicolons,
  parentheses, ellipses, or one-line fragments to manufacture cadence.

## Machine and workflow residue

- **S58 Chatbot chatter:** Include greetings, offers to continue, “I hope this
  helps,” “let me know,” or a narrated handoff to the user.
- **S59 Sycophancy:** Praise the question, agree reflexively, flatter the
  reader, or call an ordinary point excellent, insightful, or important.
- **S60 Placeholder residue:** Leave bracketed instructions, dummy dates,
  template variables, TODOs, or example names in publication text.
- **S61 Citation-token leakage:** Preserve internal search, retrieval, footnote,
  attribution, or model citation markup instead of a real citation.
- **S62 Tool tracking in links:** Leave AI-tool UTM, referral, session, or
  tracking parameters on canonical links.
- **S63 Reasoning-chain leakage:** Publish “let me think,” scratch steps,
  internal plans, grading notes, or hidden decision scaffolding.
- **S64 Assistant identity residue:** Mention training data, knowledge cutoff,
  model limitations, browsing, or tools when that process is irrelevant.
- **S65 Unicode obfuscation:** Use zero-width, soft-hyphen, non-breaking-space,
  or homoglyph tricks to manipulate detectors or appearance.
- **S66 Format-copy residue:** Preserve UI labels, buttons, prompt text,
  response wrappers, or clipboard artifacts from the generating tool.

## Voice and authorship patterns

- **S67 Sudden register shift:** Move between formal polish, casual errors,
  spelling systems, or punctuation habits without a content reason.
- **S68 Generic polish:** Regularize every sentence and paragraph until the
  supplied author's bluntness, uncertainty, humor, or roughness disappears.
- **S69 Invented personality:** Add opinions, anger, delight, vulnerability,
  profanity, humor, or strong positions the author did not supply.
- **S70 Invented experience:** Add personal anecdotes, sensory details,
  reactions, incidents, users, or results to make the prose feel human.
- **S71 Artificial irregularity:** Insert fragments, one-word sentences,
  tangents, self-corrections, slang, or random punctuation only to look human.
- **S72 Confidence laundering:** Turn real uncertainty into confidence or add
  vague hedges to a well-supported claim.
- **S73 Forced stance:** Add controversy or a strong opinion to neutral
  documentation, reference material, legal text, or evidence that is mixed.
- **S74 Voice imitation without evidence:** Claim to match an author or brand
  without a usable sample or explicit voice specification.

## Default-avoid word watchlist

Replace or omit these words in most ordinary technical prose unless the word is
exact, quoted, canonical, or characteristic of the supplied author:

`beacon`, `breathtaking`, `boasts`, `bolster`, `circle back`,
`cutting-edge`, `deep dive`, `delve`, `double down`, `elevate`, `embark`,
`empower`, `ever-evolving`, `facilitate`, `foster`, `game changer`,
`game-changer`, `garner`, `harness`, `intricate`, `interplay`, `lean into`,
`leverage` (verb), `meticulous`, `multifaceted`, `must-visit`, `navigate`
(abstract challenges), `nestled`, `paradigm shift`, `paramount`, `realm`
(abstract), `renowned`, `seamless`, `showcase` (verb), `stands as`,
`state-of-the-art`, `streamline`, `supercharge`, `take a step back`,
`tapestry` (figurative), `testament` (figurative), `transformative`,
`underscore` (verb), `unpack` (analysis), `utilize`, `vibrant`,
`world-class`.

Prefer a specific fact over a synonym. For example, replace “robust” with the
supported capability, not automatically with “powerful.”

## Density-watch words

Keep one when it is the best word. Revise clusters and repeated use:

`actually`, `additionally`, `align with`, `crucial`, `dynamic`, `enduring`,
`enhance`, `ensure`, `essential`, `effective`, `fundamentally`, `highlight`
(verb), `important`, `inherently`, `innovative`, `key` (adjective),
`landscape` (abstract), `notably`, `moreover`, `furthermore`, `pivotal`,
`powerful`, `rich` (figurative), `robust`, `significant`, `valuable`,
`various`.

Inspect filler uses of `really`, `just`, `literally`, `genuinely`, `honestly`,
`simply`, `truly`, `deeply`, `interestingly`, `importantly`, `crucially`,
`inevitably`, `potentially`, `possibly`, `generally`, and `essentially`. Keep
them when they change meaning, certainty, contrast, or the author's
recognizable voice.

Inspect participles that claim analysis without supplying it: `contributing`,
`emphasizing`, `ensuring`, `fostering`, `highlighting`, `marking`,
`reflecting`, `setting the stage`, `shaping`, `showcasing`, `symbolizing`, and
`underscoring`.

## Context-only signals

Do not flag or rewrite these by themselves:

- one em dash, en dash, colon, semicolon, parenthesis, ellipsis, or fragment;
- curly quotation marks, an Oxford comma, perfect grammar, or consistent style;
- one three-item list, transition, rhetorical question, contrast, or short
  emphatic sentence;
- passive voice when the actor is unknown or immaterial;
- normal technical agency such as “the API returns” or “the command creates”;
- question headings in a genuine FAQ or task-led guide;
- title case when the destination requires it;
- neutral reference prose, formal vocabulary, or correct domain jargon;
- repeated canonical terms that prevent ambiguity;
- greetings and sign-offs in actual correspondence;
- watched text inside quotations, code, identifiers, titles, or examples; and
- a style choice established by the supplied author's samples.

Judge clusters, frequency, fit, evidence, and reader effect. The catalogue
guides editing; it does not prove authorship and must not become a detector
score.
