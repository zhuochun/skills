# Slop language watchlists

Read only for an explicit word/phrase audit or when pattern diagnosis leaves
a specific lexical uncertainty. A match is an inspection prompt, not a defect
or authorship evidence. Preserve accurate terminology, quotations, code,
identifiers, required qualifiers, and the supplied author's voice. Revise only
for a demonstrated reader effect; prefer a specific fact over a synonym.

## Default-avoid word watchlist

Replace or omit these words in most ordinary technical prose unless the word is
exact, quoted, canonical, or characteristic of the supplied author:

`beacon`, `breathtaking`, `boasts`, `bolster`, `circle back`,
`cutting-edge`, `deep dive`, `delve`, `double down`, `elevate`, `embark`,
`empower`, `ever-evolving`, `facilitate`, `foster`, `game changer`,
`game-changer`, `garner`, `harness`, `intricate`, `interplay`, `lean into`,
`leverage` (verb), `meticulous`, `multifaceted`, `must-visit`, `navigate`
(abstract challenges), `nestled`, `paradigm shift`, `paramount`, `realm`
(abstract), `renowned`, `seamless`, `showcase` (verb), `stands as`,
`state-of-the-art`, `streamline`, `supercharge`, `take a step back`,
`tapestry` (figurative), `testament` (figurative), `transformative`,
`underscore` (verb), `unpack` (analysis), `utilize`, `vibrant`,
`world-class`.

Prefer a specific fact over a synonym. For example, replace “robust” with the
supported capability, not automatically with “powerful.”

## Density-watch words

Keep one when it is the best word. Revise clusters and repeated use:

`actually`, `additionally`, `align with`, `crucial`, `dynamic`, `enduring`,
`enhance`, `ensure`, `essential`, `effective`, `fundamentally`, `highlight`
(verb), `important`, `inherently`, `innovative`, `key` (adjective),
`landscape` (abstract), `notably`, `moreover`, `furthermore`, `pivotal`,
`powerful`, `rich` (figurative), `robust`, `significant`, `valuable`,
`various`.

Inspect filler uses of `really`, `just`, `literally`, `genuinely`, `honestly`,
`simply`, `truly`, `deeply`, `interestingly`, `importantly`, `crucially`,
`inevitably`, `potentially`, `possibly`, `generally`, and `essentially`. Keep
them when they change meaning, certainty, contrast, or the author's
recognizable voice.

Inspect participles that claim analysis without supplying it: `contributing`,
`emphasizing`, `ensuring`, `fostering`, `highlighting`, `marking`,
`reflecting`, `setting the stage`, `shaping`, `showcasing`, `symbolizing`, and
`underscoring`.

## Default-avoid phrase watchlist

### Empty setup and signposting

`here's what you need to know`; `in this article, we will`; `this
comprehensive guide`; `this comprehensive overview`; `let's dive in`; `let's
explore`; `let's unpack`; `let's break this down`; `now let's look at`;
`without further ado`; `before we begin`; `first and foremost`; `when it comes
to`; `in today's world`; `in the age of`; `in the world of`; `in today's
landscape`; `in a world where`; `going forward`; `moving forward`; `with
regard to`; `in terms of`; `it turns out`; `let me be clear`; `I'll say it
again`; `can we talk about`; `here's what I find interesting`; `here's the
problem though`.

### Importance and pseudo-insight

`it's worth noting`; `it's important to note`; `it should be noted`; `the
reality is`; `the truth is`; `the real question is`; `what really matters`;
`at its core`; `in reality`; `the deeper issue`; `the heart of the matter`;
`what nobody tells you`; `what most people get wrong`; `the part everyone
misses`; `the uncomfortable truth`; `make no mistake`; `this is huge`; `this
changes everything`; `full stop`; `period`; `let that sink in`; `this matters
because`; `here's why that matters`; `at the end of the day`.

### Inflated significance

`serves as a testament`; `stands as a testament`; `serves as a reminder`;
`plays a vital role`; `plays a crucial role`; `marks a pivotal moment`;
`represents a major shift`; `a key turning point`; `underscores the
importance`; `highlights the significance`; `reflects a broader`; `symbolizes
the ongoing`; `leaves an indelible mark`; `deeply rooted`; `setting the stage
for`; `shaping the future`; `contributing to the`; `evolving landscape`.

### Vague authority and notability

`experts agree`; `experts argue`; `research shows`; `research suggests`;
`studies show`; `many believe`; `many argue`; `observers note`; `critics say`;
`industry reports suggest`; `several sources`; `it is widely believed`;
`widely regarded as`; `independent coverage`; `leading expert`; `featured in`;
`profiled in`; `has been cited in`; `active social media presence`.

### Copula avoidance and weak verb phrases

`serves as`; `stands as`; `marks`; `represents`; `boasts`; `features`;
`offers`; `has the ability to`; `makes use of`; `provides users with the
ability to`; `is designed to enable`; `plays a role in`.

Use these only when they express more than `is`, `has`, `uses`, or the direct
action.

### Formulaic contrast and engagement

`not only X but Y`; `not just X`; `not merely X`; `it's not X, it's Y`; `not
this, not that`; `the question isn't X`; `what if I told you`; `think about
it`; `plot twist`; `the catch?`; `the kicker?`; `the brutal truth?`; `sound
familiar?`; `honestly?`; `look`; `here's the thing`; `the thing is`; `let's be
honest`; `real talk`; `and that's okay`; `not always, not perfectly`.

### Filler and hedging

`in order to`; `due to the fact that`; `at this point in time`; `in the event
that`; `for the purpose of`; `it is important to`; `it is essential to`; `in
the context of`; `there are several ways to`; `there are a few things to
consider`; `in general`; `generally speaking`; `it could potentially`; `it
might perhaps`; `one might say`; `there is a sense that`; `to some extent`;
`in some ways`.

### Business and workshop jargon

`navigate the challenges`; `unpack the analysis`; `lean into`; `double down`;
`take a step back`; `moving forward`; `circle back`; `on the same page`; `deep
dive`; `feature, not a bug`; `dressed up as`.

### Generic challenge and future language

`despite its`; `faces several challenges`; `despite these challenges`;
`challenges and legacy`; `future outlook`; `looking ahead`; `the road ahead`;
`poised for growth`; `the future looks bright`; `exciting times lie ahead`;
`continues its journey`; `a step in the right direction`; `ongoing
initiatives`; `continued success`.

### Recaps and empty explanation

`in conclusion`; `in summary`; `to sum up`; `overall`; `all in all`; `in
other words`; `put simply`; `simply put`; `that is to say`; `as mentioned
above`; `as we have seen`; `whether you`; `whether it's`; `this section
covers`.

### Meta-commentary and performative emphasis

`hint`; `spoiler`; `you already know this, but`; `but that's another post`;
`the rest of this essay explains`; `in this section, we'll`; `as we'll see`; `I
want to explore`; `I promise`; `they exist, I promise`; `this is genuinely
hard`; `this is what leadership actually looks like`; `this is what X actually
looks like`; `actually matters`.

### Vague declaratives

`the reasons are structural`; `the implications are significant`; `this is the
deepest problem`; `the stakes are high`; `the consequences are real`.

### Chatbot and workflow residue

`I hope this helps`; `Of course!`; `Certainly!`; `Absolutely!`; `Great
question!`; `You're absolutely right`; `That's an excellent point`; `You raise
an important issue`; `Would you like me to`; `Want me to`; `Should I
continue`; `Let me know if`; `Here is an overview`; `Let me walk you through`;
`Let me think`; `Breaking this down`; `First, I'll`; `As an AI`; `up to my
last training update`; `as of my knowledge cutoff`; `based on available
information`.

### Speculative privacy filler

`specific details are limited`; `not extensively documented`; `not publicly
available`; `maintains a low profile`; `keeps personal details private`;
`prefers to stay out of the spotlight`; `it is believed that`; `likely grew
up`; `appears to have`.
