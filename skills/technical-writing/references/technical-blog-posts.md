# Technical Articles and Blog Posts

Use this branch when authorial communication matters in addition to technical
accuracy. Treat the article as communication, not as canonical documentation
for an interface, procedure, or support commitment.

## Bound the article

- Name the intended reader and one useful promise.
- State whether the reader should understand a mechanism, perform a task, or
  evaluate an experience-backed claim. Load the corresponding documentation
  branch when that function must also succeed.
- Bound the promise by inspected sources, system, version, environment, and
  supplied experience.
- Route unresolved product, architecture, or policy decisions instead of
  writing as if they were settled.

## Ground the prose

- Start from inspected sources, code, results, or supplied experience.
- Pair important abstractions with a concrete example and connect the example
  back to the general claim.
- For a technology-adoption or possibility story, foreground the person's goal,
  stakes, and consequential agency; show technology changing the feasible action
  space rather than acting as the protagonist. Foreground the technology when
  safety, consent, surveillance, displacement, power, or material tradeoffs
  require scrutiny.
- Distinguish observation, sourced material, synthesis, inference, and opinion.
- Never invent personal experience, results, benchmarks, quotations, or
  reactions.
- Carry enough context for a reader who cannot interrupt the author.
- Preserve observable vocabulary, rhythm, humor, and formality from supplied
  samples. Prefer concrete nouns and verbs, and inspect cadence when rhythm
  matters.
- Link changing technical facts to their canonical documentation.

## Finish the article

- Check consequential claims, code, commands, outputs, versions, and links
  against inspected evidence; label anything not verified.
- Remove material that does not improve understanding or trust.
- Preserve qualifications and uncertainty while editing for clarity.

Complete the article when a target reader can state its promise, understand the
mechanism or lesson, inspect consequential support, and recognize its limits.
