## Bounded synthesis

1. **Answer.** State what the evidence supports for the framed question.
2. **Material evidence.** Present the smallest set of claims that carries the conclusion, with exact provenance.
3. **Contradictions and alternatives.** Preserve credible counterevidence, incompatible contexts, and alternative explanations.
4. **Applicability.** State where the result transfers and where it does not.
5. **Confidence.** Explain source quality, independence, consistency, directness, recency, and important access gaps without a pseudo-precise score.
6. **Use boundary.** State whether the synthesis is sufficient for the declared purpose and which downstream owner retains the decision.
7. **Next probe or stop.** Name only research that could materially change the answer; otherwise record why the search stopped.

## Durable research state

Persist only when repeated use or reconstruction cost justifies it:

- stable identities or retained copies of raw sources where permitted;
- exact evidence addresses and source-version metadata;
- derived claims kept separate from source-owned text;
- superseded, retracted, inaccessible, and excluded sources;
- unresolved questions and failed searches worth preserving; and
- owner, freshness expectation, and pruning or recheck trigger.
